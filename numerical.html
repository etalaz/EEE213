Numerical problem 1: diode–resistor operating point

Method: Newton–Raphson. Code: newton_diode.py.

Model and assumptions

A voltage source Vs drives a resistor R in series with a diode. The unknown is the diode voltage Vd. Assumptions: ideal Shockley diode, constant temperature (about 300 K), steady state (DC), no series resistance inside the diode.

QuantityValueUnit
Source voltage Vs5V
Resistance R1000Ω
Saturation current Is1×10−12A
Ideality factor n1dimensionless
Thermal voltage VT0.02585V

Equations

Diode current (Shockley): Id = Is (exp(Vd / (n VT)) − 1) in A. Resistor current: Ir = (Vs − Vd) / R in A. Kirchhoff's current law requires Id = Ir, so the unknown Vd is the root of

f(Vd) = Is (exp(Vd / (n VT)) − 1) − (Vs − Vd) / R = 0 (A)

Derivative derived by hand

Differentiating term by term with respect to Vd:

f′(Vd) = (Is / (n VT)) exp(Vd / (n VT)) + 1 / R (A/V)

The Newton–Raphson step is Vdk+1 = Vdk − f(Vdk) / f′(Vdk), starting from Vd0 = 0.6 V and stopping when the step is below 10−12 V.

Result after 6 iterations (count): Vd = 0.574147 V, Id = 4.425853 mA. The step shrinks roughly as the square of the previous step, as expected for quadratic convergence.

Independent check

Two methods that do not use the derivative give the same operating point.

MethodVd (V)Id (mA)
Newton–Raphson0.5741474.425853
Closed form with the Lambert W function0.5741474.425853
Brent bracketing root finder on [0, 5] V0.5741474.425853

The difference between Newton–Raphson and the Lambert W solution is below the printed precision (0.00 V in the program output). Physical check: the resistor drops 5 − 0.574147 = 4.425853 V, and 4.425853 V / 1000 Ω = 4.425853 mA, equal to the diode current. A silicon diode near 0.57 V at a few milliamperes is also plausible.
